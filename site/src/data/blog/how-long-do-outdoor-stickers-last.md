The honest range is three weeks to about eight years. That spread is not vague
supplier hedging — it is the real distance between an uncoated paper sticker on
a sunlit window and a laminated cast vinyl decal on a shaded van panel. Four
decisions account for nearly all of it: the facestock, the laminate, the ink
system, and how carefully the thing was applied.

Worth clearing up one piece of vocabulary first, because it causes more ordering
mistakes than any other.

## Waterproof and weatherproof are different claims

Waterproof describes the material. A waterproof facestock does not absorb water,
soften, cockle or delaminate when it gets wet. Vinyl, polypropylene and
polyester all qualify straight out of the box. Coated paper does not, whatever
the product photo suggests.

Weatherproof describes the whole assembly surviving outdoor conditions over
time, which is a much harder test. It has to hold up against ultraviolet light,
freeze-thaw cycling, thermal expansion, abrasion and standing water at the
edges — all at once, for years. Every weatherproof sticker is waterproof. Plenty
of waterproof stickers fail badly outdoors within one summer. When you are
sourcing, our [waterproof sticker material grades](/product/waterproof-stickers/)
are the right starting point for wet indoor use such as bottles, tumblers and
bathroom packaging, while genuine outdoor exposure calls for the full
weatherproof stack described below.

## What actually destroys an outdoor sticker

Ultraviolet light does most of the damage, and it works on two layers at once.
It fades pigment, and it embrittles the facestock polymer so it cracks instead
of flexing. This is why a sticker facing south dies in roughly half the time of
the same sticker facing north, and why anything mounted flat — a bumper, a
picnic table, a machine housing — ages fastest of all. Horizontal surfaces take
close to double the annual UV dose of vertical ones.

Water rarely attacks the face. It attacks the edges, wicking under the adhesive
at any lifted corner, then freezing and levering the corner further open. One
lifted edge on a die cut shape will unzip the whole sticker across a winter.
Precision cutting matters here for a reason people underestimate: our
[die cut waterproof stickers in custom shapes](/product/die-cut-water-proof-stickers/)
are kiss-cut cleanly through the face without nicking the liner, which leaves a
sealed, uncrushed edge rather than a frayed one.

Thermal cycling is the quiet third factor. A dark sticker on a car panel can
swing from below freezing to 160 °F in a single day. Every cycle expands and
contracts the film against a substrate expanding at a different rate, working
the adhesive bond loose at the perimeter.

## Lifespan by material

The numbers below assume vertical outdoor exposure in a temperate US climate,
with a matching UV laminate. Subtract roughly 40% for horizontal mounting or
high-UV states like Arizona and Florida.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Facestock | Typical outdoor life | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coated paper | 2–6 weeks | Short events, indoor-adjacent only |
| Monomeric calendered vinyl | 1–3 years | Flat surfaces, budget signage, short campaigns |
| Polymeric calendered vinyl | 3–5 years | Windows, equipment, simple curves |
| Cast vinyl | 5–8 years | Vehicles, compound curves, long-life branding |
| Polyester (PET) | 3–7 years | Industrial asset tags, chemical exposure |

</div>

Calendered and cast are worth understanding rather than memorising. Calendered
vinyl is rolled out of a heated mass, which leaves internal stress in the film;
over time it wants to shrink back toward its original dimensions, pulling away
from edges and corners. Cast vinyl is poured as a liquid onto a casting sheet
and cured with no stretching, so it has almost no memory and stays put on
curves. That single difference explains most of the price gap between
[cast and calendered vinyl stock](/product/custom-vinyl-stickers/), and why
vehicle work is quoted differently from flat panel work.

## The laminate is not an upsell

An overlaminate is a clear film applied on top of the printed face, and it is
the single highest-return line on an outdoor quote. It typically adds one to
three years by taking the UV hit before the ink does, and it absorbs abrasion
from brushes, weather and hands.

Gloss laminate sheds water and dirt slightly better and holds colour depth.
Matte cuts glare, which matters on anything read at an angle or photographed,
at a small cost in saturation. Neither is more durable in any meaningful sense,
so the decision is aesthetic — the trade-offs are covered properly in our guide
to [choosing between matte and gloss finishes](/blog/matte-vs-glossy-stickers-which-finish-to-choose/).

Ink matters alongside it. UV-cured, latex and eco-solvent inks are all built for
outdoor life; standard aqueous inkjet is not, and will fade visibly in a single
season regardless of what it is printed on.

## Application decides more than people expect

A premium film applied carelessly will underperform a budget film applied well.
Three rules cover most failures.

- **Clean properly.** Isopropyl alcohol, not household glass cleaner. Ammonia and silicone-based cleaners leave a film that adhesive cannot key into.
- **Respect the temperature window.** Apply between roughly 50 °F and 90 °F. Below that the adhesive is too stiff to wet out the surface; above it, it grabs before it can be positioned.
- **Allow the bond to cure.** Acrylic adhesives reach only part of their final strength on contact and continue building for 24 to 72 hours. No pressure washing, no car wash, no rain if you can avoid it.

Surface energy is the invisible factor. Glass, painted steel and powder-coated
metal are high-energy surfaces that adhesive wets easily, which is why
[window stickers applied to glass](/product/custom-window-stickers/) reliably
outlast the same design on a plastic bin. Polypropylene, polyethylene and
textured powder coats are low-energy and need an aggressive adhesive
specification or they will lift at the corners within months.

Vehicles deserve their own note. Fresh paint needs 30 days to gas off before
anything is applied over it, and recessed or heavily curved panels need cast
film rather than calendered. Our
[car stickers for painted body panels](/product/custom-car-stickers/) are
specified with that in mind, since a decal that shrinks off a door recess after
one winter is a warranty conversation nobody wants.

## Getting the specification right first time

Match the material to the exposure honestly. A three-month promotion does not
need eight-year cast vinyl, and a permanent fleet marking should never be quoted
on monomeric film. If the sticker also has to be seen after dark — trailers,
helmets, equipment, safety marking — that is a different material class again,
and [reflective film for low-light visibility](/product/reflective-stickers/)
solves it far better than a bright colour ever will.

Tell your supplier three things when you brief the job: what surface it goes on,
whether it faces up or sideways, and how many years it has to survive. Our
[outdoor stickers built for weather exposure](/product/outdoor-stickers/) are
quoted against exactly those answers rather than a generic durability promise.
