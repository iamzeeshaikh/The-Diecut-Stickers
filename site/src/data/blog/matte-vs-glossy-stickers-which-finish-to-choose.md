Almost every custom sticker order ends at this question, usually after
everything else is settled. It is worth more thought than it usually gets,
because finish changes how the printed colours read, whether the design is
legible under a shop light, and how the sticker looks after a month of handling.
What it does not change is the price — matte and gloss laminates cost
effectively the same, so this is a design decision, not a budget one.

One clarification before the comparison. The finish is a thin clear layer over
the print, either a laminate film or a liquid coating. It sits on top of the
ink, whatever the facestock underneath happens to be. That means the same
[vinyl sticker stock in either finish](/product/custom-vinyl-stickers/) can be
ordered matte or gloss without changing anything about its durability or
adhesive.

## The optical difference in one paragraph

Gloss reflects light specularly: rays bounce off at a consistent angle, like a
mirror. Matte reflects diffusely, scattering light in every direction from a
microscopically rough surface. That single difference cascades into everything
else. Because a gloss surface sends stray light away rather than back at the
viewer, more of the light reaching your eye has actually passed through the ink,
so blacks look deeper and colours look more saturated. Matte scatters some light
back before it ever reaches the pigment, which lifts the blacks slightly and
compresses the contrast range.

Printers measure this in gloss units. High gloss reads above 80 GU, satin sits
around 30 to 50, and a true matte laminate comes in under 15. If a supplier
offers "semi-gloss" without a number, they mean satin.

## Where gloss wins

Colour depth is the headline. If the artwork is photographic, has gradients, or
leans on rich blacks and deep brand colours, gloss will render it closer to the
proof. The difference on a solid black is obvious side by side — matte black
reads as dark charcoal.

Gloss is also the correct choice for anything with a specialty effect underneath
it, because those effects work by reflecting light in a controlled way. Matting
them defeats the point.
[Holographic sticker film](/product/custom-holographic-stickers/) relies
entirely on specular reflection to produce its rainbow shift, and a matte
overlaminate scatters that into a dull grey shimmer.

The same logic applies to foil and metallic work.
[Metallic foil sticker effects](/product/custom-metallic-stickers/) need a
polished surface to read as metal rather than as flat grey ink.

Practically, gloss also cleans up better. The tighter surface sheds dirt and
grease, and it resists staining from coffee, oil and adhesive residue — useful
on anything applied in a kitchen, workshop or retail environment.

## Where matte wins

Glare is the deciding factor most of the time. A gloss sticker under a retail
spotlight or direct sun produces a hot reflection that can wipe out a whole
section of the design at the wrong viewing angle. Matte has no hotspot at any
angle, which is why it dominates on product packaging photographed for
e-commerce, on anything read at arm's length, and on labels applied to curved
surfaces where some part of the curve will always be catching a light source.

Text-heavy designs read measurably better in matte. Ingredient panels, care
instructions, batch codes, QR codes and small type all benefit from a surface
that never throws a reflection across a line of copy. The lower contrast costs
you nothing when the design is black type on a light background.

Matte also has a tactile advantage that is hard to quantify and easy to notice.
It feels soft and slightly warm rather than slick, which is why it has become
the default across skincare, coffee, craft spirits and small-batch food. Paired
with an uncoated facestock it reinforces a natural look, though bear in mind
that a plastic laminate compromises any compostability claim — worth reading
alongside our guide to
[eco-friendly and biodegradable sticker materials](/blog/eco-friendly-and-biodegradable-sticker-materials/)
if that matters to your brand.

One more practical point: matte accepts pen and ink. If anyone needs to write a
date, a batch number or a name on the sticker after it is applied, gloss will
smear and matte will not.

## The honest drawbacks of each

Matte shows fingerprints more readily. Skin oils sit in the microscopic texture
and darken it, and on a dark matte sticker that handled look develops quickly.
Gloss wipes clean.

Gloss shows scratches more readily. Fine abrasion that a matte surface hides
completely appears on gloss as a visible haze, particularly on dark colours.

Neither finish is meaningfully more durable outdoors. Both laminates carry UV
inhibitors, both protect the ink underneath, and lifespan is set by the
facestock rather than the sheen. The choice is aesthetic even on a five-year
vehicle decal.

## Satin and soft-touch

Satin splits the difference at around 40 GU: most of gloss's colour depth with
most of matte's glare control. It is the safe answer when a design mixes
photographic imagery with body copy, and it is underused simply because it is
rarely offered by name.

Soft-touch is a matte laminate with a velvet texture engineered into it. It is a
premium, noticeably tactile finish that suits cosmetics and luxury packaging. It
is also the most fragile of the group — it marks easily and does not belong on
anything handled roughly.

## A short decision list

- **Photographic artwork, deep blacks, rich colour** — gloss
- **Small type, ingredient panels, QR codes** — matte
- **Retail shelf under spotlights, or product photography** — matte
- **Holographic, metallic or foil effects underneath** — gloss
- **Kitchen, workshop, or anywhere it gets wiped down** — gloss
- **Natural, craft or minimal brand positioning** — matte
- **Someone has to write on it** — matte
- **Genuinely undecided** — satin

Transparent stock is its own case. A clear sticker is meant to disappear into
the surface it sits on, and a matte laminate frosts the film enough to break
that illusion, so
[clear stickers on glass and bottles](/product/custom-clear-stickers/) are
almost always finished gloss.

## Order a sample before you commit

Screens cannot show sheen. A design that looks correct on a monitor can land
either way in the hand, and the gap is widest on dark colours and on anything
with fine detail. Print the same artwork both ways, look at it under the light
it will actually live under, and the decision usually makes itself in seconds.
